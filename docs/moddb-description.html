This is a fork

Vintage Symphony was made by Haviland, with contributions from DoodleGarbage. All of the music, the situation model, and the idea itself are theirs. This fork exists only because the original had not been updated for Vintage Story 1.22, and it carries their work forward with their name on it.

Every change here is offered back. If Haviland wants any of it — or all of it — it is theirs to take, and I will help merge it. If Haviland would rather this fork did not exist, say so and I will take it down, no questions asked.

What it does

Vintage Symphony replaces the game's music engine with one that pays attention to what you are doing. It reads where you are and what is happening — how far from home, how deep underground, whether something is hunting you, whether you have been hurt, whether a temporal storm is running — and picks music to match, from a pack of nearly 70 instrumental tracks.

What is different in this fork

  • Runs on Vintage Story 1.22.
  • The music is a folder of files, not a second mod. No companion mod to keep in step, and nothing that stops the game loading if it goes missing.
  • Nothing is downloaded unless you ask for it. The old version fetched a 200MB music pack on its own the first time you played, and phoned home on every world join. Now there is a Download button, and it is the only thing that reaches the network.
  • Music can come from anywhere. Paste an address into the config dialog and it becomes a source you can download, update and remove. Several can be on at once.
  • Your own tracks are easy. Drop .ogg files in a folder and the mod writes a manifest for you to edit.
  • The game's own music is just another source, at the top of the list, on or off like the rest.
  • Music eases between situations instead of cutting to the next track the moment a score crosses. The original meant to do this — the smoothing was there but was being handed milliseconds where it wanted seconds, so it never had any effect.
  • It costs a fraction of what it used to. Two things dominated: it read every block in a 37-block cube several times a second looking for a resonator, and it traced a line of sight to every creature nearby. Both were doing far more work than the answer needed.
  • Installing or updating a music source takes effect straight away, without restarting.

Getting the music

Install the mod, load a world, and open the config with .music config. Press Download next to "Vintage Symphony music". It is about 200MB and comes from the original author's release page.

The music lives in ModData/vintagesymphonyforked/sources/ inside your Vintage Story data folder, one folder per source.

The game's own music follows the game's own rules — village tracks play in villages, and so on. The game also marks each track for survival, creative or both; the config dialog has a switch for whether to keep that split. Off, the default, all of them play in either mode.

About the music

The pack above is royalty free, and every track in it is credited at the bottom of this page. Anything else you point this mod at is on you: check what its licence asks for, honour it, and keep the credits with the files.

Someone wrote that music. They recorded it, mixed it, and then decided you could have it — that is a gift, not an absence of an owner. If you can buy their work, buy it. If you cannot, credit them by name, link them where people will see it, and tell someone else about them. Please do not scrape tracks into a pack and pass them around; the reason there is so much good music free to use is that musicians keep choosing to make it so, and that only holds while it is treated well.

Adding your own music

Make a folder for it under ModData/vintagesymphonyforked/sources/ — say my-tunes/music/ — and put your .ogg files in it. Start the game once and the mod writes a tracks.json next to them listing everything it found. Edit it to say when each track should play:

{
  "tracks": [
    { "file": "quiet-morning.ogg", "situations": ["calm", "idle"] },
    { "file": "charge.ogg", "situations": ["fight"], "title": "Charge!" }
  ]
}

The situations are calm, idle, adventure, cave, keep, danger, fight, dead and temporalstorm. A track can list as many as it likes. title is optional and is what .music info shows.

keep is a room you built under the ground — sealed the way a cellar is, below the natural terrain. Inside one the cave music stops, whether or not you have keep tracks; with them, they play there and nowhere else. The game sizes rooms at fourteen blocks a side, so a hall bigger than that reads as open.

A pack using the game's own musicconfig.json format still works exactly as before, and is the way to go if you want the finer controls — time of day, season, temperature, sunlight.

A mod can ship music the same way: put tracks.json and the files under assets/<modid>/music/ and anyone who installs the mod has the music, no editing needed. The full guide is in docs/adding-music.md in the repository.

Console commands

  • .music info — what is playing
  • .music next — skip to another track, and end any pause
  • .music stop — stop the music and hold silence for a few minutes
  • .music config — the music sources dialog
  • .music debug — the situation overlay, if you want to see it thinking
